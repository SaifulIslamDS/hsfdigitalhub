01 · Website Content & Editorial Guidelines

Purpose and Website Role

hsfbd.org is HSF's primary public website. It should help a visitor understand who HSF is, what it does, what evidence supports the work, and how to engage responsibly.

Core principle: Public content should be useful, accurate, dignified, current and easy to verify.
  • Present HSF's institutional identity clearly.
  • Explain current projects and verified results.
  • Provide reports, policies and public accountability information.
  • Support appropriate volunteering, partnership and donation actions.
  • Keep demonstration or internal systems separate from the public website.

04 · Website Content & Editorial Guidelines

Current-Site Content Priorities

The present website has strong authentic photography and useful history, but the content needs a clearer institutional structure.

  • Use project consistently instead of program.
  • Separate current project information from historical activity updates.
  • Reconcile public statistics by scope and reporting period.
  • Reduce repeated text on E4BL and A2PHC location sections.
  • Strengthen About, Impact, Resources and accountability content.
  • Review identifiable child sponsorship profiles through safeguarding and consent controls.
  • Keep ShurjoPay as the primary online payment gateway and simplify the public donation experience.
  • Review plugin, theme, update and security dependencies before redesign deployment.

06 · Website Content & Editorial Guidelines

Homepage Content Standard

The homepage should explain HSF before asking for support. Use one strong hero, concise project cards, verified impact, a story, current resources and clear calls to action.

  1. Hero: Human Safety Foundation (HSF) + clear value proposition.
  2. Short “Who We Are” introduction.
  3. Four flagship project cards.
  4. Verified impact figures with reporting dates.
  5. Story of Change or field evidence.
  6. Where We Work.
  7. Latest News & Insights.
  8. Reports and accountability links.
  9. Volunteer, partner and support actions.
07 · Website Content & Editorial Guidelines

Project Page Standard

Use one consistent template for every flagship project.

Project name → Why it exists → Who it serves → What HSF does → Locations → Current reach → Results → Story → Reports → Get involved
  • Use the full project name before the acronym.
  • Separate current reach from cumulative reach.
  • Use approved project images and evidence.
  • Keep location detail concise; use cards or a map instead of repeating the same paragraph.
  • Move historical camps or events to News / Project Updates.
08 · Website Content & Editorial Guidelines

About, Governance and Leadership

The About section should build institutional trust without long biography-style text.

  • Who We Are: 100–150 words.
  • Mission: one clear statement.
  • Vision: one clear statement.
  • Values: 4–6 practical values.
  • History: short timeline.
  • Governance: approved roles and short biographies.
  • Registration & Recognition: verified institutional facts only.
  • Policies & Accountability: links to approved public documents.

11 · Website Content & Editorial Guidelines

Resources and Transparency

Create one trusted library for approved public materials.

  • Annual reports and audited/approved public summaries.
  • Project reports and results briefs.
  • Public policies: safeguarding, privacy and other approved governance documents.
  • Publications and research.
  • Training and knowledge resources.
Transparency means appropriate verified disclosure—not publishing confidential internal documents.
12 · Website Content & Editorial Guidelines

Get Involved and Donation Content

Use clear pathways for volunteers, partners, CSR teams and supporters. Donation content should build trust before asking for money.

  • Keep ShurjoPay as the primary online payment gateway.
  • Show only management-approved organizational payment routes.
  • Explain receipt, privacy, refund/cancellation and donation-support contacts.
  • Avoid making a donation feel like a WooCommerce product purchase.
  • Review individual “Sponsor a Child” profiles; prefer project- or scholarship-based support where possible.

Contact Page Standard

Keep the page simple but route enquiries clearly.

  • General enquiries.
  • Partnership / CSR.
  • Volunteer.
  • Media.
  • Donation support.
  • Office address, map and office hours.
  • Accessible contact form with a short privacy note.

15 · Website Content & Editorial Guidelines

Terminology and Human Dignity

Describe the challenge without reducing a person to the challenge.

  • Use project, not programme, in HSF operational content.
  • Use Human Safety Foundation (HSF) on first institutional mention; use HSF afterwards.
  • Use “participant”, “student”, “patient” or “community member” when more precise than “beneficiary”.
  • Avoid labels such as “helpless”, “poor people” or other wording that defines people only by hardship.
  • Use child- and disability-related terminology respectfully and consistently.
16 · Website Content & Editorial Guidelines

Statistics and Public Data Standard

Do not publish an orphan statistic—a number without context.

Minimum public format: value + metric + project/scope + reporting period + current/cumulative meaning.

Keep one approved impact-data register so the website, social media, reports and proposals use the same public figure.

17 · Website Content & Editorial Guidelines

Stories of Change and Case Studies

A case study is evidence of change, not poverty marketing.

Context → Challenge → HSF response → Change → Evidence → Person's voice → Wider meaning → Consent
  • Choose identifiable, anonymized or composite formats deliberately.
  • Never present a composite story as one real named person.
  • Do not use unnecessary private family, health or safeguarding details.
  • Let the person's agency and goals remain visible.
18 · Website Content & Editorial Guidelines

Images, Media and Safeguarding

Use authentic HSF photography where consent and safeguarding allow. Images should support evidence, not manufacture it.

  • Record project, date, location, photographer/source, consent and public-use approval.
  • Use descriptive filenames and useful alt text.
  • Do not publish a child's full name, patient diagnosis, exact home address or sensitive details in filenames, alt text or captions.
  • Remove unnecessary GPS metadata from sensitive field images where practical.
  • Do not use stock or AI imagery as if it were evidence of HSF field activity.
19 · Website Content & Editorial Guidelines

Website SEO and Image SEO

SEO should help people find useful HSF information. It should not change the writing into keyword-heavy copy.

  • One clear page purpose and one descriptive H1.
  • Unique page title and meta description.
  • Short, stable URLs and meaningful internal links.
  • Descriptive image filenames and alt text.
  • Canonical URLs, sitemap and structured data for the public site where appropriate.
  • Monitor search visibility and unexpected indexed pages.

Accessibility and Language

Target WCAG 2.2 AA for new and redesigned public pages.

  • Logical heading hierarchy and keyboard-friendly navigation.
  • Visible focus states and sufficient contrast.
  • Meaningful link text; avoid “click here” where possible.
  • Alt text, captions or transcripts for important media.
  • Do not rely on colour alone to communicate meaning.
  • Use human-reviewed Bangla and English. Do not publish raw machine translation.

22 · Website Content & Editorial Guidelines

AI-Assisted Content

AI may support drafting, editing, translation, metadata and structure. It must not become the factual source.

  • Human review is required before publication.
  • Verify names, dates, statistics, quotations, medical/legal claims and partner references.
  • Do not upload confidential patient, student, HR, financial or safeguarding data to an unapproved AI service.
  • Do not use AI to fabricate beneficiary stories, quotations, project evidence or field photographs.

Editorial Workflow, Review and Content Lifecycle

Need → Brief → Evidence → Draft → Fact check → Safeguarding/privacy → Brand/editorial → SEO/accessibility → Approval → Publish → QA → Review

Every page should have a lifecycle: Create → Publish → Review → Update → Archive/Redirect.

ContentReview approach
Evergreen pagesScheduled review at least annually or when facts change.
Project pages / impact figuresReview with each approved reporting cycle.
Governance / contactUpdate as soon as an approved change occurs.
Campaign / eventClose, archive or redirect when no longer active.
