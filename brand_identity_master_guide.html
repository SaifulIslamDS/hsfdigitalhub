06 · Logo governance

The official logo does not follow campaign colour

The final stack intentionally does not trace or recreate the HSF logo.

Open detailed reference

The final stack intentionally does not trace or recreate the HSF logo. Every editable template contains an OFFICIAL_LOGO_PLACEHOLDER group so the approved vector master can be inserted.

  • To use the approved HSF vector logo in AI, SVG, EPS or vector PDF form.
  • To preserve proportions and clear space.
  • Not to recolour the HSF logo simply because a campaign uses Blue, Gold, Red or Violet.
  • A blue/red/gold/violet HSF logo should only be created if management formally approves a rebrand.
  • The campaign palette may change; the organization’s identity must remain recognizable.
Operational rule

It is recommended to place the official logo into the placeholder layer. It is recommended not to treat alternative campaign palettes as permission to redesign the organization mark.

08 · Brand naming & social identity

One organization name, one recognizable social identity

HSF should separate its institutional name from the shorter identity people may already recognize on social media.

Open detailed reference

HSF should separate its institutional name from the shorter identity people may already recognize on social media.

Institution: Human Safety Foundation · Preferred institutional form: Human Safety Foundation (HSF) · Short form: HSF · Established social identity/search alias: HSF NGO
Why “HSF NGO” still has value
  • Short, familiar and easy to remember.
  • Immediately signals the organization type to a new social-media visitor.
  • Preserves recognition already built around the existing social identity.
  • Can support searches from people who already know the organization as “HSF NGO”.
  • Works naturally in community-facing and informal social communication.
Where “HSF NGO” is weaker
  • “HSF” is an acronym and can refer to unrelated organizations.
  • “NGO” describes a category, not the actual organization name.
  • It is less complete in donor, partner, media, research and institutional contexts.
  • A first-time visitor may not know what “HSF” stands for.
  • Random switching among several names can fragment recognition and search signals.

Recommended naming architecture

ContextPreferred namingRule
Official website, reports, policies, proposalsHuman Safety Foundation (HSF)To use the full institutional identity first.
Partner, donor, media and professional communicationHuman Safety Foundation (HSF)To avoid relying on “HSF NGO” alone.
Established social-media display identityHSF NGO may remain where already establishedThe profile bio/About information should clearly connect it to Human Safety Foundation (HSF).
Social caption — first institutional mentionHuman Safety Foundation (HSF)To use “HSF” naturally after the first clear mention.
Short copy, badges and space-limited designHSFTo use only where the surrounding context already identifies the organization.
Search alias / social hashtagHSF NGO / #HSFNGOTo use as a supporting discovery term, not as a replacement for the official name.
Existing social handles and URLsShould remain stableChange only through a deliberate management decision with a migration plan.

Branding recommendation

Recommended approach: protect Human Safety Foundation (HSF) as the institutional brand, preserve HSF NGO as an established social identity and search alias where useful, and repeatedly connect the two.

Why this approach is stronger

  • Brand continuity: existing followers are not forced to relearn the organization overnight.
  • Institutional clarity: donors, partners and search users can see the full name behind the acronym.
  • Search consistency: the website, social profiles and public references can reinforce the same entity instead of competing variants.
  • Lower migration risk: established social URLs and handles can remain unchanged.
  • Professional flexibility: HSF can use a formal identity in institutional settings and a shorter familiar identity in social settings without creating two brands.
Do not create new unofficial variations.

It is recommended to avoid changing between forms such as “Human Safety NGO”, “HSF Foundation”, “Human Safety Foundation NGO” or other invented labels. Consistency matters more than adding extra keywords to the name.

15 · Carousel system

Cover + complete inner-page storytelling architecture

Each visual system and size includes eight coordinated carousel pages.

Open detailed reference

Each visual system and size includes eight coordinated carousel pages. A carousel can use five to eight slides, but every slide should keep the same size, orientation and palette.

01
Slide 01

Cover / Hook

Why should I swipe?

A clear hook that makes people swipe. It is recommended to keep the cover simple: one idea, one promise.

02
Slide 02

Context / Problem

What is happening?

It is recommended to state the context without sensationalism; use a short context point, a verified observation and a community implication.

03
Slide 03

Why It Matters

Why should people care?

It is recommended to connect the issue to real-life outcomes and explain the human consequence.

04
Slide 04

HSF Response

What is HSF doing?

It is recommended to explain the HSF response in three simple steps.

05
Slide 05

Evidence / Impact

What do verified data show?

It is recommended to use only verified figures and include reporting period plus source.

06
Slide 06

Human Story / Photo

What does this mean for a person or community?

It is recommended to use an approved HSF photo, a short human quote or insight and consent-checked context.

07
Slide 07

Action / Practical Steps

What can the audience do?

Give practical actions such as learn, discuss, share responsibly, volunteer, participate, partner or support.

08
Slide 08

Closing / CTA / Source

What is the next step and where is the evidence?

Close with one message, one clear next step and source or official contact when required.

Carousel rules:
  • To keep Slide 1 visually simple.
  • One slide = one idea.
  • To use page numbers such as 03 / 08.
  • Not to repeat the full logo at oversized scale.
  • To use the last slide for one CTA.
  • To keep long explanations in the caption rather than on cards.

17 · Illustrator workflow

How to turn SVG masters into working AI files

Open the SVG in Adobe Illustrator.

Open detailed reference
  1. Open the SVG in Adobe Illustrator.
  2. Open Window → Layers.
  3. Find the named groups such as logo placeholder, project badge, photo placeholder, editable content and footer.
  4. To replace the logo placeholder with the approved HSF vector logo.
  5. To replace placeholder text and photos.
  6. To keep the selected palette consistent.
  7. Save as a new .ai file and retain an editable master.
  8. Export final artwork separately as PNG/JPG or other required publication format.

Multi-artboard masters

For one theme, Illustrator can use 12 artboards for the core-post set or 8 artboards for a carousel set. All artboards in one carousel must use the same dimensions.

20 · Campaign consistency

Do not mix visual systems casually

Within one carousel, campaign or visual series:

Open detailed reference

Within one carousel, campaign or visual series:

  • To use one palette system.
  • To use consistent typography.
  • To use the same logo treatment.
  • To use the same page indicator and badge family.
  • Not to combine Gold cover + Blue inner pages + Red CTA unless the campaign has a deliberately approved colour-coded structure.
Use alternative palettes deliberately

It is recommended to choose one because the communication purpose fits trust, hope, urgency or dignity/inclusion.

Avoid random variation

A different colour for every post weakens recognition and makes HSF look like multiple unrelated brands.

21 · Image SEO, accessibility & digital assets

Make every image understandable, findable and safe

Image optimization is not only a search-engine task.

Open detailed reference

Image optimization is not only a search-engine task. For HSF it combines discoverability, accessibility, performance, brand quality, asset management and safeguarding .

1. Use descriptive filenames before upload

Website image filenames should describe the real subject in plain language. It is recommended to use lowercase words separated by hyphens. It is recommended to keep them concise and factual.

Avoid
IMG_7384.jpg
DSC00982.jpg
final-final-new2.jpg
facebook-post-1.jpg
Prefer
hsf-e4bl-classroom-dhaka-2026.jpg
hsf-primary-health-camp-dhaka-2026.jpg
hsf-climate-awareness-session-2026.jpg
hsf-women-health-awareness-2026.jpg

Note: filenames are especially useful for website asset organization and image search. Social platforms may rename uploaded files, so caption context and platform alt text matter more there.

2. Write useful alt text

Alternative text should briefly describe the meaningful content of the image in its context. It should help a person who cannot see the image and should not become a container for repeated keywords.

Practical formula

Subject + activity/context + HSF project + location only when relevant and safe

Example: “Students participating in a classroom activity at HSF's Education for Better Life project in Dhaka.”

  • The alt="" attribute should be used for genuinely decorative images that add no information.
  • Not to start every description with “image of” or “photo of” unless the format itself matters.
  • Not to copy the page title into every image alt attribute.
  • Not to repeat unrelated keywords or hashtags in alt text.
  • To describe what is important in context, not every visible object.

3. Support images with nearby text

  • To place important images near relevant headings and explanatory copy.
  • To use captions when a date, place, source or reporting context helps interpretation.
  • Not to place essential facts only inside a graphic; provide important information in readable page or caption text as well.
  • For charts and infographics, include the key finding and source in nearby text.

4. Optimize size and performance

  • Export at the dimensions actually needed rather than uploading unnecessarily huge originals.
  • To compress JPG/PNG responsibly; use modern web formats such as WebP where the publishing workflow supports them.
  • To keep text readable after compression.
  • To use responsive image dimensions where the website platform supports them.
  • Lazy-load non-critical below-the-fold images; keep logos and critical hero assets available promptly.

5. Protect privacy in image metadata

Safeguarding takes priority over SEO.
  • Not to place a child's full name, patient diagnosis, private family circumstance, phone number, exact home address or other sensitive information in filenames, alt text or captions.
  • To remove unnecessary GPS/location metadata from beneficiary and field images before public release where the workflow allows.
  • Not to use a person's identity as a keyword merely to improve discoverability.
  • Consent and safeguarding classification must be confirmed before optimization and publication.

6. Prepare social and link-preview images deliberately

  • To use a clean, branded social-preview image for important public website pages.
  • To keep the main message readable at small preview sizes.
  • Not to overload preview graphics with paragraph-length text.
  • To use the official HSF logo correctly and keep safe margins around platform crops.
  • When a platform supports custom alt text, prepare it as part of the publishing packet rather than after posting.

Recommended image publishing checklist

Before export

Correct crop · readable text · brand rules · consent · no sensitive data.

Before website upload

Descriptive filename · suitable dimensions · compression · alt text · caption/source if needed.

Before social publish

Platform crop · caption context · alt text where available · safeguarding recheck.
