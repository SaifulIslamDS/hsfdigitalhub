Communication Philosophy

No fixed calendar. No rushed publishing. Choose the most relevant theme, verify the evidence, create quality content, publish when ready, learn from the response, and continue.

HSF communication should not make people feel sorry for communities for a moment. It should help people understand why a social problem exists, how it affects dignity and opportunity, what HSF is practically doing, what evidence is available and what a thoughtful person can do next.

The empathy-to-action ladder

  1. Notice the human reality
  2. Understand the structural barrier
  3. Recognize the right or social principle involved
  4. See the practical HSF response
  5. Look for evidence and learning
  6. Reflect on personal/community responsibility
  7. Choose a realistic action
  8. Support responsibly when ready

Tone guardrails

  • Do not use pity as the main persuasion mechanism.
  • Do not portray HSF as a rescuer and communities as passive recipients.
  • Use hope that is earned by real action.
  • Use questions that invite reflection, not accusation.
  • Make public-health advice medically reviewed and practical.
  • Make rights content understandable without turning every post into legal language.
  • Never invent numbers to make a post stronger.

  • UDHR: equal dignity, health/basic wellbeing, education and non-discrimination.

  • Rights references guide communication and safeguarding; they do not turn HSF social posts into legal advice.

Detailed 48-Post Plan

The 12 thematic content areas form an open-ended framework. Each theme has three core concepts and one optional concept to use only when evidence, media, consent, approval and production capacity are ready.

Theme 01 — Education as a Right, Opportunity and Protection

Focus: Universal education, continuity, aspiration and community responsibility

SDG emphasis: SDG 4, SDG 10

Suggested visual system: Hope Gold / Evergreen

CoreProject Story / Rights Lens

W1-P1 - Education Is a Right, Not a Privilege

Project: E4BL - Education
Strategic angle: Begin with the principle that access to school is only the first step. Quality, continuity, safety, family support and the ability to keep learning are what turn enrolment into opportunity.
Key ideas:
  • Education should not depend on family income or social position.
  • Children need continuity, not one-time admission.
  • Quality education should build confidence, skills, dignity and possibility.
Reflection question: If a child has the ability to learn but not the conditions to continue, have we really given that child an educational opportunity?
Primary CTA: Notice the barriers around a child. Start one conversation that helps keep education possible.
SDG lens: SDG 4 - Quality Education; SDG 10 - Reduced Inequalities
Rights lens: UDHR Article 26; CRC Articles 28-29
Recommended format: Authentic classroom photo + Project Story; optional 5-slide carousel
Required evidence/input: Current E4BL classroom activity or approved field photo; verify any project fact or number used.
Primary success signal: Meaningful shares, saves, reflective comments
CoreAwareness Carousel

W1-P2 - What Does It Really Take to Keep a Child in School?

Project: E4BL / Cross-HSF
Strategic angle: Move beyond the simple idea of school admission. Show the invisible conditions that sustain learning: materials, attendance, parental support, protection, safe relationships, nutrition, confidence and patient follow-up.
Key ideas:
  • A child can be enrolled and still be at risk of dropping out.
  • Small practical barriers accumulate.
  • Families often need information and support, not blame.
Reflection question: Which barrier is easiest for society to ignore because it happens quietly inside a family?
Primary CTA: Share this with a parent, teacher or community member who influences a child's education.
SDG lens: SDG 4 - Quality Education; SDG 1 - No Poverty
Rights lens: CRC Articles 27-29
Recommended format: Awareness carousel using 6-8 slides
Required evidence/input: Project-reviewed list of actual continuity barriers observed by E4BL staff.
Primary success signal: Carousel completion, saves, shares
CoreHuman Story / Case Study

W1-P3 - A Dream Needs Time to Grow

Project: E4BL - Education
Strategic angle: Profile a student, anonymized when necessary, whose aspiration is ordinary but whose path requires unusual persistence. Focus on agency, effort and support rather than pity.
Key ideas:
  • Begin with the child's aspiration.
  • Describe one real barrier without exposing private details.
  • Show what helped the child continue.
Reflection question: How many dreams disappear not because children stop dreaming, but because adults stop protecting the conditions that let those dreams grow?
Primary CTA: Help create an environment where a child has time to learn, grow and choose a future.
SDG lens: SDG 4 - Quality Education
Rights lens: CRC Articles 6, 12, 28-29
Recommended format: Human Story template / portrait carousel
Required evidence/input: Structured interview, guardian/child consent where appropriate, teacher verification.
Primary success signal: Read time, shares, empathy-driven comments
Recommended 4thCommunity Reflection

W1-P4 - When a Child Leaves School, Whose Problem Is It?

Project: Cross-HSF
Strategic angle: Ask a serious social question without assigning blame. Explore the roles of family, school, neighbours, employers, community leaders, civil society and public systems.
Key ideas:
  • Dropout rarely has one cause.
  • Responsibility is shared across the environment around the child.
  • Awareness can begin before a crisis.
Reflection question: What could your own community do earlier - before a child disappears from the classroom?
Primary CTA: Comment with one realistic action a community can take.
SDG lens: SDG 4; SDG 16 - Peace, Justice and Strong Institutions
Rights lens: CRC Articles 3, 18, 28
Recommended format: Quote/Reflection graphic + discussion caption
Required evidence/input: No beneficiary data required; project review recommended.
Primary success signal: Meaningful comments and discussion quality

Theme 02 — Childhood, Protection and Voice

Focus: Child rights, child labour, violence, early marriage and listening to children

SDG emphasis: SDG 5, SDG 8, SDG 16

Suggested visual system: Dignity Violet / Trust Blue

CoreThought Leadership / Awareness

W2-P1 - Childhood Is Not Just Preparation for Adulthood

Project: E4BL / Child Rights
Strategic angle: Remind audiences that childhood itself has value. Children need education, play, protection, voice and development now, not only because they will become productive adults later.
Key ideas:
  • A child is a person with rights today.
  • Education, play and safety are connected.
  • Development should not be reduced to future economic productivity.
Reflection question: Do we value children for who they are now, or only for what we hope they will become later?
Primary CTA: Protect time for children to learn, play, ask questions and grow safely.
SDG lens: SDG 4; SDG 16
Rights lens: CRC Articles 3, 6, 12, 31
Recommended format: Quote / institutional message + authentic child-safe image
Required evidence/input: Safeguarding-approved image; no personal story required.
Primary success signal: Shares, comments, save rate
CoreAwareness Carousel

W2-P2 - When Work Steals a Childhood

Project: E4BL / Child Rights
Strategic angle: Discuss child labour with empathy for families under economic pressure while making clear that harmful work can damage education, health and development.
Key ideas:
  • Avoid blaming poor families.
  • Explain how short-term income pressure can create long-term loss.
  • Point to the need for social support, education continuity and safer adult livelihoods.
Reflection question: What kind of economy makes a family feel that a child's labour is necessary for survival?
Primary CTA: Challenge harmful child labour without shaming families. Support solutions that protect both children and household dignity.
SDG lens: SDG 8.7 - End child labour; SDG 4; SDG 1
Rights lens: CRC Article 32
Recommended format: Awareness carousel
Required evidence/input: Project/legal review; avoid unsupported local prevalence claims.
Primary success signal: Saves, shares, thoughtful comments
CoreRights Awareness

W2-P3 - Listen Before Deciding for Children

Project: Cross-HSF / Child Rights
Strategic angle: Build awareness around children's right to be heard in matters affecting them. Connect listening with education, safeguarding, family decisions and project design.
Key ideas:
  • Listening does not mean children make every decision alone.
  • Age-appropriate participation builds trust and confidence.
  • Adults remain responsible for safety and best interests.
Reflection question: When was the last time we asked a child what they think before making a decision about their life?
Primary CTA: Ask one child an age-appropriate question today - and listen without interrupting.
SDG lens: SDG 16 - Inclusive societies
Rights lens: CRC Article 12; CRC Article 3
Recommended format: Human-centred awareness graphic / short Reel
Required evidence/input: No identifying child data; safeguarding review.
Primary success signal: Comments, shares, community discussion
Recommended 4thAwareness / Protection

W2-P4 - Early Marriage Does More Than End Schooling - It Shrinks Choice

Project: E4BL + Women Empowerment
Strategic angle: Frame early marriage as a loss of education, health, agency and future choice. Use careful, non-accusatory language that invites families and communities into prevention.
Key ideas:
  • Education continuity is a protective factor.
  • Girls need information, voice and supportive adults.
  • Community norms can change through respectful dialogue.
Reflection question: What choices should every girl be able to make before others decide her future for her?
Primary CTA: Support girls to stay in education and participate in decisions that shape their lives.
SDG lens: SDG 5.3 - End child marriage; SDG 4
Rights lens: CRC Articles 12, 19, 28; best-interests principle
Recommended format: Awareness carousel / Dignity Violet
Required evidence/input: Safeguarding and project review; no exploitative imagery.
Primary success signal: Shares, saves, serious comments

Theme 03 — Primary Healthcare Before Crisis

Focus: Accessible primary care, prevention, health literacy and frontline service

SDG emphasis: SDG 3

Suggested visual system: Trust Blue

CoreProject Story / Current Activity

W3-P1 - Healthcare Should Be Reachable Before It Becomes an Emergency

Project: A2PHC - Healthcare
Strategic angle: Use a current camp or consultation activity to explain why primary care closer to communities matters. Focus on access, early attention, continuity and referral rather than event photography alone.
Key ideas:
  • Primary care is often the first point of contact.
  • Early attention can identify needs before they become more difficult to manage.
  • Responsible care includes referral when a case is beyond the service scope.
Reflection question: How many health problems become harder simply because care was too far away, too expensive or delayed?
Primary CTA: Encourage people to seek qualified health advice early and use responsible local services.
SDG lens: SDG 3 - Good Health and Well-being
Rights lens: UDHR Article 25; CRC Article 24
Recommended format: Trust Blue Project Story + field photos/Reel
Required evidence/input: Latest verified A2PHC activity, location, approved service figures, consent-cleared media.
Primary success signal: Reach, health enquiries, shares
CorePublic Health Awareness

W3-P2 - Prevention Is Quiet, but Powerful

Project: A2PHC - Healthcare
Strategic angle: Create physician-reviewed practical guidance on a common preventive health issue observed in HSF communities. Keep it simple and actionable.
Key ideas:
  • Use one topic only.
  • Explain what people can do, when to seek care and what not to ignore.
  • Avoid diagnosis or individualized medical advice.
Reflection question: Why do we often value treatment more visibly than prevention, even when prevention asks less from us?
Primary CTA: Save this post and share it with someone who may benefit from the information.
SDG lens: SDG 3
Rights lens: CRC Article 24; right to health information
Recommended format: 5-7 slide awareness carousel
Required evidence/input: Medical reviewer approval and current guidance from qualified HSF physicians.
Primary success signal: Saves, shares, completion rate
CoreExplainer / Health Literacy

W3-P3 - What Can a Primary Health Visit Actually Do?

Project: A2PHC - Healthcare
Strategic angle: Demystify primary healthcare: history-taking, basic examination, advice, common-condition management within scope, referral and follow-up. Avoid promising services HSF does not provide.
Key ideas:
  • Primary care is not “less important” care.
  • The goal is appropriate first-contact care.
  • Referral is a sign of responsible practice, not failure.
Reflection question: Would more people seek care earlier if they better understood what primary healthcare can and cannot do?
Primary CTA: Ask questions during a consultation and follow referral advice when given.
SDG lens: SDG 3.8 - Universal health coverage
Rights lens: Right to accessible health information and care
Recommended format: Explainer carousel / icon-led design
Required evidence/input: A2PHC service model confirmed by health team.
Primary success signal: Saves, health literacy engagement
Recommended 4thPeople Behind HSF

W3-P4 - Care Is a Journey, Not a Camp

Project: A2PHC - Healthcare
Strategic angle: Profile a doctor or supervisor and show the planning, travel, consultation, records, referral and follow-up behind a field service day.
Key ideas:
  • Humanize the professional work.
  • Show preparation and responsibility.
  • Focus on continuity rather than heroism.
Reflection question: What does “showing up” repeatedly for a community require from a health worker?
Primary CTA: Recognize the professionals who bring responsible care closer to people.
SDG lens: SDG 3; SDG 17
Rights lens: Health and dignity lens
Recommended format: Portrait/Reel / Behind-the-scenes
Required evidence/input: Short staff interview, role verification, approved photo/video.
Primary success signal: Trust, comments, professional/volunteer interest

Theme 04 — Health, Water, Sanitation and Dignity

Focus: WASH, menstrual health, healthy environments and the education-health connection

SDG emphasis: SDG 3, SDG 5, SDG 6

Suggested visual system: Trust Blue / Dignity Violet

CoreAwareness

W4-P1 - Clean Water Is Health Infrastructure

Project: Public Health / Climate / Community
Strategic angle: Treat safe water as foundational to health, dignity and daily life, not as a background issue. Connect household practice, community systems and responsible water use.
Key ideas:
  • Water quality affects everyday wellbeing.
  • Safe storage and hygiene matter alongside infrastructure.
  • Local participation matters for sustainable WASH practice.
Reflection question: When clean water is unreliable, which parts of daily life become harder first - health, school, work or dignity?
Primary CTA: Protect safe water practices at home and support community responsibility for shared water sources.
SDG lens: SDG 6 - Clean Water and Sanitation; SDG 3
Rights lens: CRC Article 24 - health, water and safe environment
Recommended format: Trust Blue awareness carousel
Required evidence/input: Health/team review of practical WASH advice; no unverified infrastructure claims.
Primary success signal: Saves, shares, practical engagement
CoreAwareness / Dignity

W4-P2 - Menstrual Health Is Health, Education and Dignity

Project: Women Empowerment / Health
Strategic angle: Discuss menstrual health without shame. Connect accurate information, products, sanitation, privacy and supportive attitudes with participation in school and community life.
Key ideas:
  • Use respectful language.
  • Avoid presenting menstruation as a problem to hide.
  • Include boys/men as allies when appropriate.
Reflection question: How much participation is lost when a normal biological process is surrounded by silence or stigma?
Primary CTA: Make conversations about menstrual health factual, respectful and supportive.
SDG lens: SDG 3; SDG 5; SDG 6
Rights lens: Dignity, health, education and non-discrimination
Recommended format: Dignity Violet awareness carousel
Required evidence/input: Health and safeguarding review; verified project activity if linked to HSF.
Primary success signal: Saves, shares, constructive comments
CoreThoughtful Practical Awareness

W4-P3 - Health Advice Only Works When People Can Practice It

Project: Public Health
Strategic angle: Challenge overly simplistic public-health messaging. Acknowledge that clean water, safe housing, money, time, transport and family conditions shape whether people can follow advice.
Key ideas:
  • Avoid blaming individuals for structural barriers.
  • Give low-cost realistic options where possible.
  • Recognize when broader support or services are needed.
Reflection question: Before telling someone what they “should” do, have we asked whether they actually have the means to do it?
Primary CTA: Give health advice with empathy for people's real circumstances.
SDG lens: SDG 3; SDG 6; SDG 10
Rights lens: UDHR Article 25; equality and dignity lens
Recommended format: Quote/reflection + carousel
Required evidence/input: Public-health review; practical examples from field staff without identifying individuals.
Primary success signal: Comments, shares, trust
Recommended 4thCross-Project Insight

W4-P4 - A Healthy Child Is Better Able to Learn - and a Learning Child Is Better Able to Protect Health

Project: E4BL + A2PHC
Strategic angle: Show the two-way connection between education and health literacy without making unsupported causal claims about HSF results.
Key ideas:
  • Health affects attendance and concentration.
  • Education can strengthen understanding of health information.
  • Integrated social support sees the whole child.
Reflection question: Why do we often separate education and health when children experience them as one life?
Primary CTA: Think beyond project labels. Support conditions that help children learn and stay healthy together.
SDG lens: SDG 3; SDG 4
Rights lens: CRC Articles 24, 28-29
Recommended format: Split-screen infographic / cross-program carousel
Required evidence/input: Project review; use illustrative principles unless HSF has verified linked data.
Primary success signal: Saves, shares, cross-program understanding

Theme 05 — Climate Action as Human Safety

Focus: Climate resilience, local responsibility, children and follow-through

SDG emphasis: SDG 13, SDG 11

Suggested visual system: Evergreen

CoreThought Leadership / Project Story

W5-P1 - Climate Change Is Not Only an Environmental Story - It Is a Human Safety Story

Project: Climate Action
Strategic angle: Connect climate risk to health, livelihoods, water, displacement, heat, children and community resilience. Keep the message grounded in HSF's local scope.
Key ideas:
  • Climate impacts are experienced through daily life.
  • Vulnerability is unequal.
  • Local awareness and preparedness matter alongside larger policy action.
Reflection question: If climate risk changes whether people can stay healthy, learn, work or live safely, can we still call it only an environmental issue?
Primary CTA: Connect climate responsibility with the people whose daily safety is most affected.
SDG lens: SDG 13 - Climate Action; SDG 11 - Sustainable Communities
Rights lens: CRC Article 24; CRC Article 29 environmental responsibility
Recommended format: Evergreen photo-led project story / carousel
Required evidence/input: Current climate activity or approved community environmental image.
Primary success signal: Shares, saves, thoughtful discussion
CoreImpact / Accountability

W5-P2 - What Happens After We Plant a Tree?

Project: Climate Action
Strategic angle: Shift from event photos to follow-through. Ask about species, location, protection, maintenance, survival checks, community ownership and learning.
Key ideas:
  • Planting is a beginning, not an outcome.
  • Maintenance should be part of the story.
  • Transparency can include what did not work.
Reflection question: Do we measure environmental action by what we did today - or by what is still alive and useful later?
Primary CTA: Support climate action that includes follow-up, maintenance and learning.
SDG lens: SDG 13; SDG 15 - Life on Land
Rights lens: Safe environment and intergenerational responsibility lens
Recommended format: Before/after or follow-up impact carousel
Required evidence/input: Verified follow-up records; do not claim survival rates without evidence.
Primary success signal: Saves, credibility, partner interest
CoreIntergenerational Awareness

W5-P3 - Children Will Inherit the Climate Decisions Adults Make Today

Project: Climate + Child Rights
Strategic angle: Use a child-rights lens without putting children in distress imagery. Connect environmental education, preparedness and adult responsibility.
Key ideas:
  • Children have less power over decisions but face long-term consequences.
  • Climate education belongs in learning.
  • Communities can model responsible habits now.
Reflection question: What climate decision are we making today that a child will still be living with years from now?
Primary CTA: Make one household, school or community practice more climate-responsible this content cycle.
SDG lens: SDG 13; SDG 4.7
Rights lens: CRC Articles 24 and 29
Recommended format: Thoughtful quote/photo or carousel
Required evidence/input: Safeguarding-approved photo; no speculative claims about individual children.
Primary success signal: Shares, comments, youth engagement
Recommended 4thPractical Community CTA

W5-P4 - Resilience Is Built Before the Crisis

Project: Climate Action / Community
Strategic angle: Focus on simple preparedness and community knowledge appropriate to HSF's activities, rather than dramatic disaster imagery.
Key ideas:
  • Know local risks.
  • Protect vulnerable people.
  • Keep essential information and contacts accessible.
  • Support community communication.
Reflection question: What is one thing your community could prepare before the next heatwave, storm, flood or other local hazard?
Primary CTA: Choose one realistic preparedness action and discuss it with your family or neighbours.
SDG lens: SDG 13.1; SDG 11
Rights lens: Right to safety and protection lens
Recommended format: Checklist carousel
Required evidence/input: Local hazard advice reviewed by responsible project staff; avoid emergency instructions beyond expertise.
Primary success signal: Saves, shares, practical responses

Theme 06 — Poverty, Inequality and Dignity

Focus: Structural barriers, equal access and non-stigmatizing social support

SDG emphasis: SDG 1, SDG 10, SDG 16

Suggested visual system: Trust Blue / Evergreen

CoreSocial Reflection

W6-P1 - Poverty Is Not a Lack of Dreams - It Is a Lack of Choices

Project: Cross-HSF
Strategic angle: Challenge stereotypes that equate poverty with lack of ambition. Connect financial hardship with constrained choices in education, healthcare, housing and time.
Key ideas:
  • People make decisions within constraints.
  • Short-term survival pressure can narrow long-term options.
  • Support should expand agency rather than replace it.
Reflection question: How different would our judgments be if we understood the choices people never had?
Primary CTA: Replace assumptions with curiosity. Ask what barrier is limiting a person's choices.
SDG lens: SDG 1 - No Poverty; SDG 10
Rights lens: UDHR Articles 1 and 25
Recommended format: Institutional reflection + authentic community image
Required evidence/input: No personal poverty story unless consented; project review.
Primary success signal: Shares, thoughtful comments
CoreCross-Project Awareness

W6-P2 - Delaying School or Healthcare Can Look Cheaper Today - and Cost More Tomorrow

Project: E4BL + A2PHC
Strategic angle: Explore how families under financial pressure may postpone education materials, transport or healthcare. Avoid unsupported economic figures; focus on the logic of delayed access.
Key ideas:
  • Small costs can become barriers.
  • Delays may compound problems.
  • Social support can protect continuity.
Reflection question: How often do we call something “a choice” when the affordable options were already removed?
Primary CTA: Support practical access - transport, information, continuity and responsible referral - not only one-time assistance.
SDG lens: SDG 1; SDG 3; SDG 4
Rights lens: UDHR Articles 25-26
Recommended format: Split infographic / carousel
Required evidence/input: Field-informed examples, generalized and anonymized.
Primary success signal: Saves, shares, donor understanding
CoreRights Awareness

W6-P3 - Equal Rights Do Not Always Mean Equal Access

Project: Human Rights / Cross-HSF
Strategic angle: Explain the difference between a right existing in principle and people having practical access to it. Use education and healthcare examples.
Key ideas:
  • Distance, cost, disability, gender and social status can affect access.
  • Equity may require different forms of support.
  • Non-discrimination is active, not passive.
Reflection question: If everyone is offered the same thing but some cannot reach it, is the opportunity really equal?
Primary CTA: Look for barriers, not only formal availability.
SDG lens: SDG 10 - Reduced Inequalities; SDG 16
Rights lens: UDHR Articles 1-2; CRC Article 2
Recommended format: Thought-leadership carousel
Required evidence/input: No individual data required; use clear examples.
Primary success signal: Saves, comments, institutional engagement
Recommended 4thEthics / Communication

W6-P4 - People Are Partners in Change - Not Pictures of Need

Project: Cross-HSF
Strategic angle: Explain HSF's dignity-first communication principle. Beneficiaries should not be reduced to images that make donors feel sorry.
Key ideas:
  • Consent matters.
  • Context matters.
  • People have strengths, choices and voices.

Social Search and Content Discoverability

Social discoverability is related to SEO but is not identical to website SEO. The goal is to make each post easy for people and platform search systems to understand: who HSF is, what the post is about, which project or issue is involved, where it is relevant, and why it matters.

Discoverability does not guarantee reach. Clear naming, useful keywords, alt text and relevant hashtags can improve findability and context, but platform reach still depends heavily on content quality, audience relevance, retention, interaction, timing and platform-specific systems.

Search-friendly caption architecture

The opening lines should carry real meaning, not only a slogan. Use natural language that identifies the subject and context before moving into reflection or emotion.

Use keywords naturally

  • Write complete sentences for people first.
  • Use the exact project name where it improves understanding.
  • Use issue terms that match the actual content rather than a list of popular words.
  • Do not repeat “NGO”, “charity”, “best NGO”, “Bangladesh NGO” or similar phrases unnaturally.
  • Do not add unrelated trending topics merely to gain impressions.

Hashtag guideline

Hashtags should support classification and recognition; they should not replace meaningful caption text. Use a small relevant set rather than a long block of generic tags.

  • Brand: #HumanSafetyFoundation, #HSFNGO where useful.
  • Project: use a project-specific tag only when it will be reused consistently.
  • Issue: choose relevant terms such as #EducationForAll, #ChildRights or another topic-specific tag.
  • Location/campaign: add only where it improves context.
  • A practical working range is usually a few carefully selected hashtags rather than dozens; adapt to the platform and content.

Social image discoverability and accessibility

  • Prepare descriptive alt text when the platform provides that feature.
  • Do not depend on text inside an image to carry the whole meaning; the caption should explain the important context.
  • Use authentic HSF photography where consent and safeguarding allow.
  • Keep social graphics readable on mobile and avoid paragraph-length image text.
  • Use a descriptive internal filename before archiving/uploading, even if the platform later renames the file.
  • Do not include a child's full name, patient information or sensitive circumstances in filenames, alt text, captions or hashtags.

Recommended discoverability packet for every post

Content fields: primary topic · intended audience · project/theme · preferred brand naming · key natural-language terms · location if appropriate.
Media fields: approved image/video · consent status · internal filename · alt text · caption/source · platform crop.

Practical HSF recommendation

Use Human Safety Foundation (HSF) to establish institutional clarity, retain HSF NGO as an established social identity/search alias where useful, and let each caption describe the real project or social issue in plain language. Better discoverability should come from clarity and relevance—not from keyword repetition.

Safeguarding and Accuracy

  • Never invent project statistics.
  • Never publish a child's identifying story without appropriate permission and safeguarding review.
  • Never expose patient records, diagnoses, prescriptions, addresses or private contact information.
  • Do not use poverty, illness or childhood vulnerability as visual shock material.

  • Partner logos require authorization.

Recommendations

  1. Treat the 12 themes as a content curriculum, not a deadline calendar. Reorder when real field evidence is stronger.
  2. Keep 3 posts as the guaranteed quality standard. Publish the fourth when there is a real reason, not to fill space.
  3. Make at least one post every theme genuinely useful even to someone who never donates.
  4. Make reflection questions specific enough to invite thought but safe enough not to solicit sensitive disclosures.
  5. Rotate between rights, practical awareness, real HSF work, evidence and human stories so the page does not feel repetitive.
  6. Use SDG labels only when the content clearly connects to the goal. Do not decorate every graphic with many SDG icons.
  7. For education and health, show continuity and follow-up. One-day activity photos should not become the whole story.
  8. For climate content, publish follow-up evidence whenever possible. “What happened after?” should become a recurring HSF question.
  9. For donor attraction, publish evidence and transparency before asking. Let trust accumulate.
  10. For LinkedIn, prioritize evidence, partnerships, publications, governance and professional stories instead of reposting every Facebook update.
  11. Build a content evidence archive so one field activity can become a social post, case study, website article, donor evidence and annual-report asset.
  12. Review v2.0 after 12 thematic content areas or after enough posts are published to identify clear audience patterns.
  13. Use Human Safety Foundation (HSF) for institutional clarity and keep HSF NGO as an established social identity/search alias where appropriate.
  14. Make the first lines of important captions descriptive enough to identify the real issue, HSF project and context without keyword stuffing.
  15. Prepare image filename, alt text and safeguarding information as part of the content packet rather than as an afterthought.
