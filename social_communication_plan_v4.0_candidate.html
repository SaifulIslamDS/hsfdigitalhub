Philosophy and Non-Negotiables

Real Work. Real People. Verified Evidence. Meaningful Communication.

HSF communications explain what occurs, why it matters, what credible evidence shows, and what HSF or others can responsibly learn or do. Posts must be useful, emotionally truthful without exploitation, accurate, respectful, non-blaming and programme-aligned.

Keep E4BL, A2PHC and Climate Action & Resilience as the approved programmes. Preserve the institutional visual identity, existing brand palettes, logo, names, typography and production components. Distinguish activities, outputs, outcomes and impact. Never imply causal impact on the basis of an isolated testimonial.

Use clear, conceptual Bangla and natural English when bilingual communication is appropriate. Maintain established plain-language and punctuation preferences. No fabricated visuals presented as genuine field documentation.

Mandatory Field Stories, Case Studies and Challenges

Field case study

Context → expressed need → HSF documented action → observable evidence → what remains or was learned. Record occurrence, date, location specificity and safeguards.

Progress/success

Starting condition → support → dated comparison → justified change statement → limitations/next step. Do not treat attendance as proof of learning improvement or contacts as unique persons.

Challenge and response

Constraint → people affected → attempted response → result OR unresolved barrier → honest lesson. Never require a happy ending or invent “overcame”.

Community voice

Individual testimony with permission and precise attribution of what the person said. It is neither a population estimate nor automatic evidence of programme-wide impact.

These four are required storytelling capabilities and ongoing editorial considerations, not compulsory weekly quotas. Safeguarding and explicit subject consent alone are not enough if publication itself creates foreseeable harm.

Use the case-study intake template internally; do not commit completed, personal case-study forms to this public repository.

Issue → Practical Response: an open-ended editorial method

Identify a documented public issue, explain who is affected and why, distinguish evidence from interpretation, then offer feasible, technically reviewed responses. Issue and response may be separate posts or combined in one carousel; neither must follow a legacy sequence or fixed quota. This is one of many reusable methods, not a privileged post bank.

Start with programme relevance and current verified research or real HSF experience. Avoid assigning responsibility to families or communities and never promise that a proposed response will solve every case.

Evidence and Publication Gate

The Approved Information Layer is the sole bridge between confidential operations and public outputs. Evidence classification and publication approval are different fields. Statuses such as RAW, CLEANED, VERIFIED, CALCULATED, ESTIMATED and INFERRED must remain explicit; editorial “approved idea” is never permission to publish a statistical claim.

  1. Identify editorial purpose, responsible programme and intended audience.
  2. Retrieve original source/authorized field occurrence record; preserve citation location, version and provenance.
  3. Extract exact claim and denominator, geographical coverage, period, population, method and uncertainty. Reproduce calculations; check for newer replacement sources.
  4. Confirm meaning: activity versus output versus outcome versus impact; observation versus testimony versus measured result; consultation contacts versus people.
  5. Review technical/clinical issues and evidence-based proposed response.
  6. Check privacy, dignity, consent, protection of vulnerable people, copyright, minors and patient anonymity. Reject publication when harm cannot be mitigated.
  7. Review visual/chart integrity (axes, units, full legend, source line, mobile readability, accessibility) and complete caption.
  8. Record named management approval for the exact public version; only then label claim `APPROVED_PUBLIC` where publication classification permits.
  9. Publish, record link/version, moderate feedback, monitor corrections and superseded values, archive published assets.
Do not store consent forms, raw patient histories, identifiable learners, staff HR details, case files or restricted ERP exports inside GitHub, the Knowledge Hub or the KDI bank. Publication of aggregate indicators requires its own verified and approved record.

Use the content-and-evidence register specification and template for future implementation. The repository contains schema and blank public-safe templates only; do not upload populated individual-level case records here.

Operational ERP: personal, clinical, financial and transactional data, role-based access, original operational activity records. Knowledge Hub: policies, evidence definitions, approved public-information governance, editorial taxonomy and templates. Public Platform: only approved publication assets and statistics.

Do not connect GitHub static JSON directly to confidential ERP APIs. Future authenticated integration must expose approved, privacy-reviewed information through a server-authorized interface with audit trail.

Measurement, Review and Correction

Communication activity ≠ programme outcome. Record editorial mix/coverage, approval timeliness, evidence-source completeness, safeguarding incidents, corrections, reach and relevant engaged audiences, quality questions, website referrals, partner inquiries and constructive community feedback.

Maintain a monthly editorial review and quarterly institutional-learning summary. Track both streams and programme representation separately without imposing an equal distribution or a fixed publication volume. LinkedIn, Instagram, Facebook and website performance should be differentiated.

When credible findings change or an error is discovered, correct/withdraw the graphic and caption, record the original/public URL, preserve version history and document the communication to affected audiences if appropriate.
